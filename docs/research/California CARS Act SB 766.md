# California CARS Act (SB 766): what the DMS must do

Research date: 2026-11-27. **Not legal advice.** Read the chaptered text on leginfo and have a California dealer attorney review the rule values before any real deal goes through the DMS.

## 1. The law in one paragraph

SB 766, the **California Combating Auto Retail Scams (CARS) Act**, was signed **October 6, 2025** (Chapter 354, Statutes of 2025) and became **operative October 1, 2026** (Civ. Code §1784.28). It adds Civil Code **§§1784.20–1784.44** (§§1784.24–1784.27 do not exist). It also replaces Civ. Code §2982 and §2985.8, Vehicle Code §11709.2 and Revenue & Taxation Code §6012.3 with new versions, and repeals Vehicle Code §11713.21. That last section was the old paid **2-day contract cancellation option** for used cars under $40,000. In its place, used-car buyers get a **free 3-day right to cancel** on used vehicles priced **$50,000 or less**. The law also requires total-price disclosure, statements that add-ons are optional, a ban on charging for add-ons that give the buyer no benefit, and **2-year** retention of compliance records.

## 2. Scope (Civ. §1784.31)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who | Licensed California dealers selling or leasing light-duty vehicles at retail |
| "Motor vehicle" excludes | wholesale sales; vehicles not required to be registered; fleet sales (more than one vehicle, for business use); commercial purchasers buying 5+ vehicles a year; GVWR ≥ 10,000 lb |
| "Used vehicle" | VC §665 definition; **motorcycles excluded** from the 3-day right |
| Not covered by the 3-day right | new vehicles; price > $50,000; lease to a lessee already in possession (§1784.43(h)); auction sales complying with VC Div. 12 (§1784.43(i)) |
| Waivers | any consumer waiver is void (§1784.21) |
| Remedies | in addition to other law (§1784.22). There is no penalty schedule in the Act; enforcement runs through UCL (up to $2,500 per violation), FAL, CLRA, DMV/BAR license action and the $50,000 dealer bond |

## 3. Total price and advertising (§1784.31(j), §1784.41(a), (c), (d), (f), (g))

- **Total price** is the sale price **before** taxes and the fees listed in VC §11713.1(e) (registration, tire fee, smog certificate fee, finance charges, doc fee, electronic filing fee, emission test up to $50). It **includes** dealer price adjustments and items installed at the time of the ad. Rebates may not be deducted.
- It must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously:
  1. in any ad referencing a specific vehicle;
  2. in any ad with a monetary amount or financing term for a specific vehicle;
  3. **in the first written communication** about a specific vehicle or financing term (email, text, quote, credit application). Keep that communication **≥ 2 years** and give a copy on written request.
- **Monthly payment quotes:** in writing, show the **total of all payments** at that monthly amount and any downpayment or trade-in assumed (§1784.41(c)).
- **Payment comparisons:** in writing, state that lower monthly payments often increase the total cost (§1784.41(d)).
- Does not apply to auction sales (§1784.41(g)).
- Sold vehicles: withdraw the ad (VC §11713(c)); this gives a safe harbor for the availability misrepresentation in §1784.40(d).

## 4. Prohibited misrepresentations (§1784.40(a)–(m))

The dealer may not misrepresent: costs or terms of buying, financing or leasing; any cost, limitation or benefit of an add-on; whether the deal is a lease or a sale; availability at the communicated total price; preapproval or guarantees; financing application information; whether the dealer keeps the downpayment or trade-in, charges fees, or starts legal process if the deal isn't finalized; trade-in payoff (VC §11709.4); the remedy if the dealer fails to sell at the total price; government or military affiliation; repossession; moving the vehicle out of state or out of the country; or any disclosure the title requires.

## 5. Add-ons (§1784.31(a), §1784.41(b), §1784.42)

- **Add-on** means any product or service not provided or installed by the manufacturer that the dealer charges for.
- **Optional disclosure:** when an add-on is represented in writing during negotiation, the dealer must state in writing, at least once and clearly and conspicuously, that the add-on **is not required** and the vehicle can be bought or leased without it. DMV OLIN 2026-10 says to do this **verbally and in writing, in at least 12-point type**.
- **No-benefit add-ons may not be charged** (§1784.42(a)):
  1. nitrogen tire fill under 95% purity;
  2. a product that doesn't cover the vehicle, the consumer or the transaction;
  3. GAP not compliant with Civ. §§2982, 2982.2, 2982.12 (including the loan-to-value limits);
  4. a service contract void for preexisting conditions (prior crash or flood damage, or a known mechanical problem);
  5. oil changes for an EV;
  6. catalytic converter marking on a vehicle without one;
  7. a surface protection product that voids the manufacturer's paint warranty.
- **Provider payment:** the dealer pays the add-on provider **within 10 days** of signing, unless a written agreement allows later payment and coverage is not affected (§1784.42(b)).
- Charging for a chosen add-on that would benefit the buyer is fine, even if it is never used (§1784.42(c)).

## 6. The 3-day right to cancel (used, ≤ $50,000) — §1784.43, §1784.31(g), (i)

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Eligibility | used vehicle (not a motorcycle), retail, price ≤ **$50,000**, not an excluded transaction |
| Period | **3 calendar days starting the day after execution** (contract signing, not delivery). If day 3 is a day the dealership is closed to the public, it extends to the next open day. Ends at **close of business** on the last day |
| Mileage | no right if the vehicle has been driven **more than 400 miles** (so exactly 400 still qualifies) |
| Restocking fee | **1.5% of the sale price, minimum $200, maximum $600**. If a shipping fee was charged, the dealer may keep its actual shipping cost instead, capped at the same amount, and refund the rest |
| Mileage fee | if driven more than 250 miles, add **$1 per mile over 250, max $150** |
| Collection | may be collected at exercise or deducted from the refund |
| Exercise | buyer **personally delivers**, during business hours: the vehicle (free of new liens, same condition except reasonable wear and tear and defects not caused by the buyer), the restocking fee if not deducted, and any other cash or items received |
| Damage | the dealer must **document** any damage beyond wear and tear; claiming damage without a reasonable basis is a violation |
| Refund | **within 48 hours** of exercise; if the payment was not an immediate verified transfer (e.g. a check), within **2 business days after it verifies**, with documentation |
| Trade-in | return it if it is still held. If it was sold or title transfer started, pay the **greatest of** the agreed value, the sale price or fair market value, minus any trade-in payoff. Give an itemized receipt with the **date and time of exercise**. Show the trade-in sale document with the buyer's personal info redacted. FMV presumption: an honored written offer valid ≥ 7 days, or a pricing-guide trade-in value (VC §11950(c)(2)) |
| Violations (§1784.43(c)) | impeding cancellation; overcharging the restocking fee; withholding or late refund of the downpayment or trade-in; no trade-in receipt; unfounded damage claims; claiming the authorized person is unavailable |
| Dealer may offer more | yes (§1784.43(j)); other rescission rights are kept (§1784.43(f)) |

**Required documents**

1. A **separate document** titled **"3-Day Right to Cancel Used Car Purchase or Lease"** (§1784.43(b)) with:
   1. dealer and buyer names;
   2. vehicle description and VIN;
   3. the deadline to cancel and return;
   4. that the dealer cannot charge for the right, but may charge a restocking fee, and how both parts of the fee are calculated;
   5. the 400-mile limit;
   6. the personal-delivery requirements;
   7. that the dealer may require signatures to carry out the cancellation;
   8. the trade-in "greater of" rule;
   9. the trade-in sale proof.
2. A **first-page contract statement** (§1784.43(e); required in the sale contract by new Civ. §2982(r)):
   > CALIFORNIA DOES NOT HAVE A COOLING-OFF PERIOD FOR NEW VEHICLES. HOWEVER, IF YOU PURCHASED OR LEASED A USED VEHICLE FOR $50,000 OR LESS, YOU HAVE 3 DAYS TO CANCEL THIS CONTRACT FOR ANY REASON. ADDITIONAL RESTRICTIONS MAY APPLY, INCLUDING A RESTOCKING FEE.
3. A **posted sign** (new VC §11709.2(a)), **at least 36-point type**, in every sales office and cubicle where terms are discussed and in every room where contracts are signed:
   > CALIFORNIA DOES NOT HAVE A COOLING-OFF PERIOD FOR NEW VEHICLES. BUT IF YOU PURCHASED OR LEASED A USED VEHICLE FOR $50,000 OR LESS, YOU HAVE 3 DAYS TO CANCEL THIS CONTRACT FOR ANY REASON. ADDITIONAL RESTRICTIONS MAY APPLY, INCLUDING A RESTOCKING FEE. ASK THE DEALER FOR MORE INFORMATION ABOUT HOW TO EXERCISE THIS RIGHT.

**Sales tax:** restocking fees under §1784.43 are excluded from gross receipts (new RTC §6012.3).

## 7. Contract changes (new Civ. §2982, operative 10/1/2026)

- The itemization no longer has a contract-cancellation-option line. The **total cash price** is the sum of (A)–(K) (§2982(a)(1)(L)).
- If the dealer charges a document processing or electronic registration fee, the contract must say **"the charge is not a governmental fee"** (§2982(n)).
- **No application fee** (§2982(o)).
- **Returned-check fee ≤ $15** (§2982(p)).
- A red-outlined box (≥ ½" × ½") showing "new" or "used" (§2982(q)).
- The 3-day statement goes in the contract (§2982(r)).
- Lease contracts: new Civ. §2985.8 carries the same changes, and the doc fee may not be represented as a governmental fee.

## 8. Language (§1784.41(b), (c)(2), §1784.43(b); Civ. §1632(b))

If the deal is negotiated **primarily in Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese or Korean**, the add-on-optional disclosure, the consideration disclosure and the 3-Day Right to Cancel document must also be given **in that language**. The DMS supports English and Spanish first. For the other three-plus languages, it records the negotiation language and **blocks the deal** if no approved translation exists.

## 9. Records (§1784.44 + related)

| Record | Minimum keep |
|---|---|
| Total-price ads and communications, including internet listings and the first written communication | 2 years from creation |
| Purchase orders and all signed finance/lease documents, plus written communications with the consumer | 2 years (but see the 7-year rule below) |
| Add-on records: contracts, GAP loan-to-value, proof of provider payment | 2 years |
| Cancellation requests, refund proof, trade-in return proof | 2 years |
| Written complaints, add-on inquiries and vehicle inquiries | 2 years |
| Dealer business records (13 CCR §272.00) | 3 years |
| Conditional sale contracts and credit documents (Civ. §2984.5) | 7 years — the Act keeps this (§1784.44(c)) |

Records may be kept in any legible form. Failing to keep them is itself a violation (§1784.44(b)). **DMS rule:** use the longest period that applies to each document, and **never delete before then**; default retention is 7 years for deal jackets.

## 10. Doc fee (unchanged)

VC §4456.5: at most **$85** if the dealer has a DMV private industry partner agreement (VC §1685), otherwise **$70**. SB 791 (2025), which would have raised it, was **vetoed**. It is not part of the total price in ads, and the contract must say it is not a governmental fee.

## 11. Federal overlay

- The FTC CARS Rule was vacated in January 2025. The FTC Act §5 and the Used Car Rule (Buyer's Guide, EN/ES) still apply.
- A California Dealer Academy article reports FTC staff pricing FAQs (Sept 15, 2026) that treat doc fees as part of the advertised price. This is **unverified**. If it holds, the DMS should show an optional "advertised price including doc fee" figure. Verify before Plan 5.
